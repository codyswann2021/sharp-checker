How to use this tool
Your step-by-step workflow for finding and validating +EV bets consistently.
The sharp bettor workflow
1
Find a potential bet on OddsJam
Open OddsJam and set your sharp book references to Pinnacle, Circa, and Prophet Exchange. Look for bets flagged as +EV — particularly on your soft target books (Fliff, Bovada, Rebet, MyBookie, Sportzino, OnyxOdds). Filter for bets where the EV% is at least +2% and the sharp-book line is meaningfully different from the target book's line.
2
Copy both sides of the sharp book lines
This is the key step. Don't just grab the line you're betting — grab BOTH sides from each sharp book. For example if you're betting the over on Pinnacle, write down what Pinnacle has the over AND the under at. You need both sides to calculate the no-vig true probability. Enter these in the "Sharp book lines" section.

Why both sides? Every sportsbook builds a margin into their lines. If they offer -110 / -110 on a coin flip, the true odds are +100 / +100. By entering both sides, the model removes that margin and finds the true implied probability — the number that actually matters.

3
For player props — enter the historical context
Switch to "Player prop" mode. Fill in what you can from the player's recent stats: L5 average, L10 average, season average, and hit rate at the line are the most important. H2H and home/away splits add more precision. You can find these in seconds on Basketball-Reference, Pro-Football-Reference, Stathead, or the OddsJam prop research tab. You don't need all fields — the model uses whatever you give it.
4
Set the right sport
Select the sport from the dropdown. This matters for the predictive model — NFL pass yards, NBA points, and MLB strikeouts have very different variance profiles. The model adjusts the probability distribution width based on sport-specific standard deviation data, which changes the true probability calculation meaningfully.
5
Read the result and size your bet
Hit "Check this bet." The verdict tells you whether to bet, the EV% tells you your theoretical edge, and the Half Kelly tells you how much to stake from your bankroll. For player props, you'll also see the model probability ring, signal bars for each input, and a convergence signal comparing the model to the sharp books.
6
Log every bet and track CLV
Every check is automatically saved to your Bet Log (persistent in this browser). After the game, note whether you won, and more importantly — check what the closing line was. If you consistently beat the closing line, your process is working regardless of short-term results. CLV is the real performance metric for sharp bettors.
Verdict guide
STRONG BET EV ≥ +3% AND model/sharp agree within 6%. Highest confidence. Size with Half Kelly.
BET EV ≥ +2%. Clear positive expected value. Bet with Half Kelly.
EDGE EV +0.5–2%. Playable edge. Use Quarter Kelly or a flat small unit.
MARGINAL EV 0–0.5%. Barely positive. Only play if you have extra model confidence.
SKIP EV below 0%. Negative expected value. Do not bet.
Bankroll rules to build consistently

The non-negotiables

  • Never bet more than Full Kelly on any single bet. Half Kelly is the professional standard.
  • No bet should exceed 5% of your total bankroll regardless of Kelly output.
  • Only chase EV, not wins. A losing bet at +4% EV is a good bet. A winning bet at -3% EV is a bad bet.
  • Your bet log is your most valuable asset. Every bet in, win or lose.
  • Don't bet a book that's already limiting you. Preserve soft-book access — it's your edge source.
  • When model and sharp lines diverge >8%, verify news (lineup, injury, weather) before betting.
  • Aim for volume over big individual bets. +EV is a law of large numbers game.

How the model works
The math and logic behind every calculation in the checker.
Two-signal architecture

The checker combines two independent probability estimates into a single composite probability. The composite probability is what drives the EV calculation and the verdict.

Signal 1 — Sharp-book consensusNo-vig line conversion from Pinnacle / Circa / Prophet
Signal 2 — Predictive modelHistorical performance + matchup + context
Composite probabilitySharp% × (1 − model weight) + Model% × model weight

Default: 70% sharp / 30% model. This is intentional. Sharp books are extremely efficient. The model adds value at the margin — especially for catching lines that haven't fully updated to recent form. You can slide the model weight to 0% to use sharp lines only.

Step 1 — No-vig conversion

For each sharp book, you enter both sides of a market (e.g. Over -120 / Under +105). The model converts each to an implied probability, adds them (they sum to more than 100% — the extra is the book's margin), then normalizes each side to remove the margin.

American to prob (negative)|odds| / (|odds| + 100)
American to prob (positive)100 / (odds + 100)
No-vig prob (side A)implied_A / (implied_A + implied_B)

Multiple books are then blended using your custom weights (Pinnacle 50%, Circa 30%, Prophet 20% by default). More weight = more influence on the consensus.

Step 2 — Predictive model (player props)

The model first calculates a weighted average of your historical inputs against the prop line:

L5 average35% weight
L10 average25% weight
Season average15% weight
H2H matchup average10% weight
Home/Away split5% weight

The spread between the weighted average and the prop line is then fed into a normal distribution (bell curve) to get a base probability. The width of that bell curve is sport- and stat-specific (see below).

Adjustments are then stacked on top: opponent defensive rank, hit rate at the line, injury/rest flag, and pace/game environment. These shift the probability up or down before the composite blend.

Sport-specific variance profiles

Different stats have very different volatility. A QB throwing for 300 yards is a much "wider" distribution than an NBA player scoring 25 points — meaning the probability curve is flatter and small edges from the model are less reliable. The checker uses calibrated standard deviation values for each sport/stat combination.

Step 3 — EV and Kelly

Expected Value measures the average outcome per dollar wagered over infinite bets at these odds.

EV formula(true_prob × win_amount) − (1 − true_prob) × stake
Full Kelly(b × p − q) / b × bankroll
Where b =decimal odds profit (e.g. -110 = 0.909)

Why Half Kelly? Full Kelly maximizes long-run bankroll growth but causes large swings that most bettors can't stomach. Half Kelly gives you ~75% of the growth with half the volatility. Quarter Kelly is for when you have lower confidence in the line accuracy.

Convergence signal

When both signals (model and sharp books) agree, confidence is highest. When they diverge, it's a warning sign — the market may be pricing in information your historical model doesn't have (recent injury news, lineup change, weather, motivation).

Strong agreement (<3% gap)Highest confidence — STRONG BET eligible
Moderate (3–8% gap)Normal — proceed as EV suggests
Divergent (>8% gap)Caution — verify news before betting
Glossary
Every term used in the checker, defined simply.

Core concepts

Expected Value (EV)The average profit or loss per dollar bet, over time. +EV means you profit long-term; −EV means you lose. Sharp betting is about finding +EV bets.
Implied probabilityWhat the sportsbook's odds say the probability of an outcome is, including their margin. -110 implies 52.4%, not 50%.
True probabilityThe actual probability after removing the book's margin (vig). This is what we calculate from sharp-book lines.
Vig / juiceThe sportsbook's built-in margin. On a -110 / -110 market, both sides pay $110 to win $100, meaning the book keeps $10 per $220 wagered (~4.5%).
No-vig lineThe fair market line with the sportsbook's margin removed. Represents what the odds "should" be at true probability.
Closing Line Value (CLV)The difference between the odds you bet at and the closing (final) odds. Consistently beating the closing line is the gold standard for proving your betting process is sharp.
Kelly CriterionA mathematical formula for optimal bet sizing given your edge and odds. Maximizes long-run bankroll growth. Most pros use Half Kelly (50% of the Kelly output) to reduce variance.

Books

Sharp bookA sportsbook that accepts sharp (winning) action and doesn't limit winners. Their lines are the most accurate reflection of true probability. Examples: Pinnacle, Circa, Prophet Exchange.
Soft bookA recreational sportsbook that prices lines less efficiently and is slower to move. This is where your edge comes from. Examples: Fliff, Bovada, Rebet, MyBookie, Sportzino.
Line shoppingComparing odds across books to find the best price. A key habit — half a point or 5 cents of juice adds up significantly over thousands of bets.
LimitingWhen a soft book restricts your max bet size because they identify you as a winning bettor. This is the goal — it means your process is working. Protect your accounts early.

Model terms

Composite probabilityThe blended probability from sharp lines and predictive model. This drives the EV calculation.
ConvergenceWhen the model probability and sharp-book probability agree closely. High convergence = high confidence in the edge.
Hit rateThe % of recent games where a player exceeded a given prop line. At 70%+ hit rate over L10, the model adds a meaningful probability boost.
Variance profileThe standard deviation used in the probability model for a given sport/stat. High-variance stats (NFL rush yards) need a bigger edge to bet confidently vs low-variance stats (NHL saves).
Model weightHow much the predictive model's probability influences the composite probability vs the sharp-book consensus. Default is 30%. Set to 0% to use sharp lines only.
